# Copywriting and conversion principles for Yugya Technologies

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Research synthesis for future copy decisions. No `app/` or `components/` files were changed.  
**Business context:** Yugya is a small Nepalese web studio serving mostly non-technical local buyers. The site is a faithful port of an owner-approved design and copy baseline, so this document is an evaluation framework, not permission to rewrite approved pages.

## How to read this document

Every finding is labelled in one of three ways:

- **Evidence-backed finding** — supported by official documentation, documented user research, or a source's stated research method. It may still need local validation before becoming Yugya copy.
- **Professional recommendation** — credible practitioner or institutional guidance that is sensible to apply but not a universal law.
- **Third-party opinion** — a vendor, case study, benchmark, or industry claim that should inform thinking without being repeated as a Yugya fact.

Source IDs such as `[S1]` refer to `docs/marketing-research/source-library.md`. External pages were paraphrased, not copied.

## Yugya's non-negotiable claim boundary

- Do not invent metrics, client counts, testimonials, logos, certifications, awards, uptime figures, rankings, or performance results.
- Keep live project claims attributable. Kohinoor Restaurant is confirmed Yugya work but must not be described as sole authorship. Samajik Abhiyaan should be presented per owner confirmation without discussing the former brand publicly.
- Keep all demo captions and `DEMO_DISCLAIMER` verbatim. Demos are sample interfaces running in the browser, not customer outcomes.
- Use only existing verified policies: written itemised quote before work, milestone billing, client-owned domains/hosting/accounts, full handover, 30 calendar days of post-launch bug fixes, no shared cPanel reselling, and a 1–2 business day response aim.
- No principle below overrides the approved design, anchor ids, brand voice, or parity contract in `AGENTS.md`.

## 1. Customer-focused copy

- **Evidence-backed finding:** Google's people-first guidance asks whether content has a real audience, demonstrates first-hand knowledge, has a clear purpose, and leaves readers able to act. Yugya already has the raw material: live client sites, founder portraits, real policies, and labelled demos. The communication task is to make those assets answer buyer questions earlier. `[S1]`
- **Evidence-backed finding:** Google's starter guide says SEO helps people understand content and decide whether to visit. That means "relevant" copy is not copy that repeats a service phrase; it is copy that helps the right visitor recognise their own problem and next step. `[S2]`
- **Professional recommendation:** CXL's value-proposition guidance stresses specificity, customer language, evidence, and testing rather than generic feature claims. Yugya should describe the customer's situation first, then the service, then the verifiable proof. `[S16]`
- **Professional recommendation:** Voice-of-customer research means collecting the words buyers use for needs, worries, and priorities, then using those words in headlines and FAQs. Yugya should mine real enquiries, founder conversations, and Search Console queries rather than inventing a "customer voice". `[S18]`

**Yugya application:** Evaluate a section by asking: "Would a restaurant owner, school administrator, or shop owner see their own situation in the first two sentences?" If the answer is no, the section is provider-centred. Use `docs/marketing-research/website-copy-audit.md` and `language-and-keyword-research.md` as the current baseline; do not treat illustrative lines in this document as approved copy.

## 2. CRO fundamentals

- **Professional recommendation:** Conversion-rate optimisation is the systematic work of helping more existing visitors take a meaningful action. CXL defines a conversion as any action that matters to the business and warns against optimising the rate at the expense of business quality. For Yugya, a qualified enquiry is more valuable than a raw form click. `[S19]`
- **Professional recommendation:** Start with research, form a hypothesis, prioritise by evidence and effort, test where traffic allows, then record the result. On a low-traffic site, ship well-supported clarity fixes and learn from interviews, enquiries, and qualitative feedback instead of forcing noisy A/B tests. `[S19]`
- **Evidence-backed finding:** Baymard's checkout research treats abandonment as a friction and trust problem: extra costs, forced account creation, payment-security worries, and long or confusing flows were among the reported reasons people stopped. The figures are ecommerce-specific and must not be quoted as Yugya metrics, but the friction pattern transfers to enquiry forms. `[S22]`
- **Professional recommendation:** Fix broken or confusing experiences before polishing headlines. A form whose status, delivery method, or next step is unclear will lose enquiries regardless of how persuasive the surrounding copy is. `[S19]`

**Yugya application:** Define the site's primary conversion per page: an enquiry on `/contact/`, a live-project visit on `/work/`, a demo launch where labelled, and a service-specific enquiry from service CTAs. Track quality in founder notes: Did the enquiry include the buyer's situation, location, and what they want to receive?

## 3. Clear headlines and testing

- **Evidence-backed finding:** Users often scan rather than read every word, so a heading must carry useful information by itself. A good heading names the topic or outcome in language the reader recognises. `[S5]`
- **Professional recommendation:** NN/g describes headings as "pick-up lines": they should help the reader choose whether to continue. Descriptive beats clever when the reader is hurried or unsure. `[S10]`
- **Evidence-backed finding:** Google says titles should be descriptive, helpful, and not exaggerated. This applies to both visible H1s and search-result title links. `[S1][S2]`
- **Professional recommendation:** A headline test should come from a hypothesis grounded in research, not from a list of adjectives. Change one meaningful idea, define the success measure first, run long enough to observe a real pattern, and keep a record even when the result is negative. `[S19]`
- **Third-party opinion:** CXL's case studies show that message changes can affect clicks and form submissions, but the reported lifts belong to those case studies and audiences. They are not forecasts for Yugya. `[S18]`

**Yugya application:** Before testing a headline, write: "Because buyers keep asking X, we believe stating Y will help them decide whether to enquire." If there is no evidence for X, collect it first. For low traffic, test by asking five local buyers which of two plain headings better explains the service, then use the result as directional evidence only.

## 4. Customer psychology and uncertainty reduction

- **Evidence-backed finding:** Interface copy helps people make decisions when it answers what something is, what happens next, and what is expected of them. Uncertainty is reduced by concrete, nearby explanations rather than by reassuring adjectives. `[S11]`
- **Evidence-backed finding:** Credibility online is closely tied to honesty, demonstrated competence, and transparency. A site that exposes its process, limits, and ownership terms can be more persuasive than one that only asserts quality. `[S9]`
- **Professional recommendation:** CXL recommends putting proof where doubt is highest — next to a claim, price explanation, form, or decision point — and removing distractions from the next step. `[S19]`
- **Third-party opinion:** Baymard's ecommerce research shows that friction and unmet expectations interrupt action. For a service site, the equivalent friction is not knowing cost drivers, ownership, aftercare, or what to send in a first message. `[S22]`

**Yugya application:** Treat these as first-time-buyer questions to answer near relevant CTAs: What will I receive? What do I need to provide? Who owns the domain and server? What will I pay later? What happens if something breaks? What do you not do? Existing verified policies already answer most of them; the copy problem is placement and plain wording.

## 5. Value propositions

- **Professional recommendation:** A useful value proposition is specific about the customer, the outcome, and the reason to believe. It avoids generic claims such as "innovative", "complete", or "world-class" when no evidence is attached. `[S16]`
- **Evidence-backed finding:** People-first content should add original value: first-hand experience, real examples, or clear analysis. Yugya's strongest differentiation is not a slogan; it is the combination of verifiable local work, honest boundaries, direct founder involvement, and client-owned infrastructure. `[S1]`
- **Professional recommendation:** Separate the value proposition from the feature list. The first sentence explains the customer outcome; the following section lists what is included, what is excluded, and how the work is verified. Technical stack belongs in support detail for readers who want it. `[S16][S17]`

**Yugya application:** Use this audit sentence, not as approved copy: "For [local business type] that needs [specific customer outcome], Yugya provides [verifiable service] with [verifiable difference], evidenced by [real project or policy]." If any bracket cannot be filled truthfully, the proposition is not ready.

## 6. Honest risk reversal

- **Professional recommendation:** Risk reversal reduces a buyer's perceived downside using real terms: clear scope before payment, defined handover, named support windows, and visible boundaries. It should never be a vague promise that everything will be perfect. `[S19]`
- **Evidence-backed finding:** Transparency supports trust. Showing what is included, what is scoped separately, and what is out of scope is more credible than hiding conditions until the sale is complete. `[S9][S11]`
- **Third-party opinion:** Guarantees and refunds can reduce friction in ecommerce, but importing that language into a service studio would create claims Yugya has not verified. Do not adopt "100% satisfaction", guaranteed rankings, guaranteed uptime, or unlimited revisions. `[S22]`

**Yugya application:** The existing risk-reversal assets are already strong: written itemised quote after scope review, milestone billing, full files/access handover, client-owned hosting accounts, 30 days of post-launch bug fixes, no shared cPanel reselling, and a 1–2 business day response aim. Future copy should make these easier to find, not add new promises.

## 7. Social proof and trust

- **Evidence-backed finding:** Trustworthy web design depends on honesty, competence, and transparency. Evidence should be specific and checkable. `[S9]`
- **Professional recommendation:** Proof should be close to the claim it supports. For a service page, that means live project links beside the statement about real work, founder portraits beside direct-access claims, and policy wording beside ownership or aftercare claims. `[S19]`
- **Evidence-backed finding:** Google's quality guidance values clear sourcing, author or organisation context, and first-hand expertise. Yugya's named founders and real project URLs are more useful than anonymous "clients say" blocks. `[S1]`
- **Third-party opinion:** Competitor research in `competitor-analysis.md` shows that local providers often use self-reported counts, superlatives, and weak ratings. Those patterns are market observations, not models to copy. `[R1]`

**Yugya application:** Build trust from what can be opened or checked: live sites, genuine screenshots, founder portraits, real URLs, written policies, and labelled demos. Do not add testimonials, logo walls, award badges, "trusted by" counts, or date-based credibility claims unless the owner supplies verifiable evidence and approves the wording.

## 8. Calls to action

- **Professional recommendation:** Give each page one primary next action and make the button name that action. "Send an enquiry", "Visit live website", "Open email draft", and "Discuss a build" are clearer than generic labels. `[S23][S17]`
- **Professional recommendation:** Landing-page guidance places the main CTA where the visitor has enough information to act, and keeps form fields to what is needed. Repeated CTAs should support the same goal, not compete with unrelated goals. `[S17][S23]`
- **Evidence-backed finding:** Interface copy reduces decision uncertainty when it explains what happens after the click. For Yugya's mailto-based form, the status must make clear that the email draft is prepared but nothing has been sent until the visitor sends it. `[S11]`

**Yugya application:** Before adding another CTA, decide whether it serves the page's primary goal. A demo page can have "Launch demo" as its main action and "Discuss a build" as a secondary action. A contact page should have one clear enquiry action, with email and location as alternatives.

## 9. Plain language

- **Evidence-backed finding:** Users scan, and unfamiliar technical vocabulary makes content harder to use. Plain language helps both novices and experts because it lowers the effort needed to understand the point. `[S5][S6][S8]`
- **Professional recommendation:** Use the words of the user's task. Government service guidance recommends short, direct language, understandable questions, and help text where a question might be ambiguous. `[S13][S14]`
- **Professional recommendation:** Keep a precise technical term once, immediately explained in ordinary words. Do not define a term using a more unfamiliar term. `[S6][R2]`

**Yugya application:** Follow `docs/marketing-research/yugya-brand-voice.md` and the vocabulary table in `language-and-keyword-research.md`. Examples of the pattern: "domain — your web address"; "hosting — the computer where your website lives"; "DNS — the settings that point your web address to the right place". Keep technical detail after the plain explanation, not before it.

## 10. Landing and service page structure

- **Professional recommendation:** Define the page's purpose, audience, and single desired action before writing. The copy, proof, form, and CTA should all serve that action. `[S23]`
- **Professional recommendation:** A strong landing sequence is: clear offer and audience → plain problem → what is included → proof near the claim → process and handover → boundaries and FAQ → final CTA. Adjust the order to the page's job; do not force every page into an identical template. `[S17][S23]`
- **Evidence-backed finding:** Search title links and snippets help people decide whether to visit. The visible page should deliver what the search result and any campaign promise led them to expect — "message match". `[S2][S17]`
- **Professional recommendation:** A service page should answer commercial questions at the point of decision: what changes the price, what happens after launch, who owns the assets, and what is out of scope. `[S13][S14][S19]`

**Yugya application:** For future service-page work, use this order: (1) who it helps and what they get, (2) common problem in plain language, (3) concrete deliverables, (4) real proof, (5) process from first message to handover, (6) ownership and recurring costs, (7) boundaries, (8) short FAQ, (9) CTA. Preserve existing ids and verified policy wording.

## 11. Writing for non-technical buyers

- **Evidence-backed finding:** Jargon excludes people who might otherwise be capable of making the decision. The problem is not intelligence; it is that the reader has not spent years inside the supplier's vocabulary. `[S6]`
- **Professional recommendation:** Start with the buyer's observable situation: "people cannot find the menu", "the school needs an online notice page", "the domain and hosting are in someone else's account". Then introduce the technical solution. `[R2][R3]`
- **Professional recommendation:** Non-technical does not mean uninterested in detail. Keep a clearly separated technical appendix, diagram, or build note for readers who want evidence, but never make it the gate to understanding the service. `[S8][R2]`

**Yugya application:** Use the audience language already documented in `language-and-keyword-research.md` and the personas in `customer-personas.md`: restaurants, schools, shops, NGOs, and small organisations. Do not open with stacks, protocol names, or workflow labels. New copy must not ask the visitor to supply a "technical brief" before they know what information is useful.

## 12. Mobile-first reading

- **Evidence-backed finding:** Mobile readers are often interrupted, distracted, and working with limited screen space. Copy should be short, scannable, and arranged so the important point appears early. `[S7]`
- **Professional recommendation:** Use descriptive headings, short paragraphs, front-loaded answers, and links or buttons that are easy to use with a thumb. Do not hide critical qualifications, prices, or disclaimers far below a visual section. `[S7][S13]`
- **Evidence-backed finding:** Google expects important content and structured data to be available to crawlers in textual form; content that depends on hidden or inaccessible components is harder to understand. `[S2][S3]`

**Yugya application:** The existing responsive design and mobile behaviour must stay intact. Copy decisions should reduce reading effort: one idea per paragraph, plain FAQ answers first, and demo disclaimers or sample-data captions never moved into an inaccessible graphic.

## 13. Local SEO and search intent

- **Evidence-backed finding:** Google's people-first guidance rewards content created for a real audience, originality, first-hand expertise, and clear purpose. SEO works best when it helps search engines understand people-first content, not when it manufactures pages for keywords. `[S1][S2]`
- **Evidence-backed finding:** Titles, snippets, descriptive URLs, internal links, images with relevant text, and crawlable content help users and search engines understand and choose pages. Keyword stuffing, copied content, and arbitrary word counts are not helpful. `[S2]`
- **Evidence-backed finding:** Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode do not require special markup or separate "AI optimisation"; standard technical access, helpful content, internal links, and accurate structured data remain the priorities. `[S3]`
- **Evidence-backed finding:** Local ranking is mainly driven by relevance, distance, and prominence. A Business Profile should be verified and kept accurate; there is no paid way to request a better local ranking. Photos, reviews, and replies can help customers evaluate a business. `[S4]`
- **Professional recommendation:** Match language to the searcher without stuffing. Current internal research supports keeping the public site in English, improving plain-English clarity, and deferring Nepali pages until Yugya has its own query and enquiry data. `[R4]`

**Yugya application:** Keep verified location wording where it already exists; do not create city-template pages. If Yugya ever verifies a Google Business Profile, keep it accurate and respond to genuine reviews. Do not claim local-pack performance, ranking positions, or review counts. For content decisions, use Search Console queries and real enquiries as the evidence base.

## 14. Ethical persuasion

- **Evidence-backed finding:** Baymard's dark-pattern research says deceptive UX may produce short-term metric gains but carries long-term trust costs. Transparent choices are safer than hidden costs, forced account creation, false urgency, disguised ads, or manipulated defaults. `[S21]`
- **Evidence-backed finding:** Credibility depends on honesty and transparency. Fake social proof, invented numbers, or hidden conditions make a site less trustworthy even if they briefly increase clicks. `[S9][S21]`
- **Professional recommendation:** Make the low-friction path the honest path: explain the quote process, show boundaries, label sample interfaces, and state what the visitor must do next. Do not use countdowns, "only today" claims, fake live counters, forced registrations, or unexplained percentages. `[S21][R5]`

**Yugya application:** Keep the mailto form's "nothing sent yet" honesty, the fixed demo disclaimer, and the boundaries table. Risk reversal must come from real policy, not pressure. If a tactic would be embarrassing when explained plainly to a shop owner across a table, do not use it.

## Practical decision checklist

Before approving any new Yugya sentence, ask:

1. Does it identify a customer situation or outcome before internal process?
2. Can a non-technical local buyer understand it on one read?
3. Is every technical term explained once in ordinary words?
4. Is proof next to the claim it supports, and can the proof be opened or checked?
5. Are ownership, aftercare, boundaries, and next steps answered nearby?
6. Does the CTA name the action and match the page's single goal?
7. Is the mobile reading order still clear without hovering or hidden content?
8. Does the page match the likely search intent without repeating keywords unnaturally?
9. Is any number, name, testimonial, logo, or performance claim verified and approved?
10. Are demos, sample data, and reference diagrams labelled exactly as required?
11. Would the wording still be honest if a competitor read it aloud?
12. Does it preserve the approved voice: plain, specific, calm, and willing to say what Yugya does not do?
