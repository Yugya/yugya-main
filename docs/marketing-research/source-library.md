# Source library — copywriting and conversion research

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Sources actually read for `docs/marketing-research/copywriting-principles.md`. No `app/` or `components/` files were changed.  
**Method note:** Web search was unavailable in this session, so authoritative pages were opened directly with `web fetch`. Only pages that returned readable content are listed. URLs that returned 404 were excluded. All external content below is paraphrased; no source passages are copied.

## Label meanings

- **Evidence-backed finding** — supported by official documentation, documented user research, or a source's stated research method.
- **Professional recommendation** — credible expert or institutional guidance, useful to apply but not a universal law.
- **Third-party opinion** — vendor, case-study, benchmark, or industry claim that should inform thinking without being repeated as a Yugya fact.

Source IDs are used in `copywriting-principles.md`.

## Google Search Central and Google Business Profile Help

### S1. Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content

- **Author / organisation:** Google Search Central, Google
- **URL:** https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content
- **Publication date:** Not shown on the fetched page
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** Google asks creators to assess whether content has a real intended audience, first-hand knowledge, a clear purpose, original value, and a satisfying outcome for readers.
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** The page describes E-E-A-T as experience, expertise, authoritativeness, and trust, with trust most important. It states that E-E-A-T is not itself a single ranking factor.
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** Google recommends considering who created content, how it was created, and why. Deceptive authorship and automation used mainly to manipulate rankings are treated as low-quality signals.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Descriptive, non-exaggerated headings and clear sourcing help both users and quality assessment.
- **One Yugya application:** Build future service content around real project evidence, named founders, verified policies, and buyer questions instead of generic keyword pages or invented claims.
- **Evidence type:** Official platform documentation
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

### S2. SEO Starter Guide: The Basics | Google Search Central

- **Author / organisation:** Google Search Central, Google
- **URL:** https://developers.google.com/search/docs/beginner/seo-starter-guide
- **Publication date:** Not shown on the fetched page
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** SEO helps search engines understand content and helps users find a site and decide whether to visit.
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** Useful pages are easy to read, well organised, original, current, and people-first. Google advises anticipating words different readers may use while writing naturally.
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** Unique, clear titles and short, page-specific snippets influence how search results are understood; keyword stuffing and copied content are discouraged.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Descriptive URLs, internal links, relevant images with nearby text, and descriptive alt text help users and crawlers. Changes take time; there is no guaranteed ranking outcome.
- **One Yugya application:** Keep service copy clear and page-specific, match visible headings to likely local buyer questions, and avoid repeating "web development company in Nepal" as a keyword-stuffing tactic.
- **Evidence type:** Official platform documentation
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

### S3. AI Features and Your Website | Google Search Central

- **Author / organisation:** Google Search Central, Google
- **URL:** https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features
- **Publication date:** Not shown on the fetched page
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** Google states that standard SEO fundamentals still apply to AI Overviews and AI Mode; there are no additional requirements or special optimisations for appearing in them.
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** Eligibility depends on normal indexing and Search eligibility. Helpful content, crawl access, internal links, page experience, textual content, supported media, and accurate structured data remain important.
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** Google says new machine-readable AI files or special schema are not required.
- **One Yugya application:** Do not create speculative "AI search" copy or markup. Continue focusing on crawlable service pages, honest content, accurate structured data, and internal links.
- **Evidence type:** Official platform documentation
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

### S4. Tips to improve your local ranking on Google

- **Author / organisation:** Google Business Profile Help, Google
- **URL:** https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091
- **Publication date:** Not shown on the fetched page
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance, and prominence. There is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** Verifying a Business Profile, keeping information complete and accurate, responding to reviews, and adding relevant photos or products can help customers evaluate a business.
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** Prominence is influenced by signals such as links and reviews; more positive reviews can help.
- **One Yugya application:** Only if Yugya has a genuine verified Business Profile, keep its service category, area, hours, photos, and review replies accurate. Never claim a local ranking position or review count that cannot be shown.
- **Evidence type:** Official product help documentation
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

## Nielsen Norman Group

### S5. How Users Read on the Web

- **Author / organisation:** Jakob Nielsen, Nielsen Norman Group
- **URL:** https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/
- **Publication date:** 1997-09-30
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** People often scan web pages instead of reading every word.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Make important information visible in headings, short sections, and front-loaded sentences so a scanner can still understand the point.
- **One Yugya application:** Put the buyer outcome and answer first in each service section; do not bury the plain-language explanation under technical detail.
- **Evidence type:** Expert UX guidance based on web-usability observation
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

### S6. Dealing with Technical or Professional Jargon

- **Author / organisation:** Kate Moran, Nielsen Norman Group
- **URL:** https://www.nngroup.com/articles/technical-jargon/
- **Publication date:** 2023-03-19
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** Specialised jargon can exclude users who otherwise understand the task.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Use the audience's own vocabulary, define necessary terms clearly, and avoid using technical language where a plain word works.
- **One Yugya application:** Explain "domain", "hosting", "DNS", "reverse proxy", and similar terms once in ordinary language before using the shorter technical label.
- **Evidence type:** Expert UX guidance
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

### S7. Reading Content on Mobile Devices

- **Author / organisation:** Kate Moran, Nielsen Norman Group
- **URL:** https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-content/
- **Publication date:** 2016-12-11
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** Mobile reading is often brief, distracted, and fragmented compared with desktop reading.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Make mobile copy concise and scannable, with the most important information early and in small units.
- **One Yugya application:** Keep FAQ answers, service intros, form guidance, and demo captions short enough to read on a phone without horizontal scrolling or careful study.
- **Evidence type:** Expert UX guidance
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

### S8. Plain Language Is for Everyone, Even Experts

- **Author / organisation:** Hoa Loranger, Nielsen Norman Group
- **URL:** https://www.nngroup.com/articles/plain-language-experts/
- **Publication date:** 2017-10-08
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** Plain language benefits expert and non-expert readers because it reduces unnecessary effort.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Clarity should not be mistaken for oversimplification; technical accuracy can remain when the sentence is direct.
- **One Yugya application:** Keep real technical detail in deliverables and build notes, but explain it with short, direct sentences so a first-time buyer can follow the decision.
- **Evidence type:** Expert UX guidance
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

### S9. Trustworthiness in Web Design: 4 Credibility Factors

- **Author / organisation:** Aurora Harley, Nielsen Norman Group
- **URL:** https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/
- **Publication date:** 2016-05-08
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** Credibility is associated with honesty, competence, and transparency, among other factors.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Sites should show evidence for claims and make organisational information, process information, and limitations understandable.
- **One Yugya application:** Use live project URLs, founder portraits, written handover terms, and visible boundaries as trust assets instead of superlatives or invented testimonials.
- **Evidence type:** Expert UX guidance grounded in credibility research
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

### S10. Headings Are Pick-Up Lines: 5 Tips for Writing Headlines That Convert

- **Author / organisation:** Hoa Loranger, Nielsen Norman Group
- **URL:** https://www.nngroup.com/articles/headings-pickup-lines/
- **Publication date:** 2015-08-09
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Professional recommendation:** Headings act as information "pick-up lines": they tell scanners what a section offers and help them decide whether to continue.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Headings should be descriptive, specific, and useful rather than merely clever.
- **One Yugya application:** Test headings by removing the body text: if the heading alone does not tell a restaurant, school, or shop owner what is relevant, rewrite it in future copy work.
- **Evidence type:** Expert UX guidance
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

### S11. Interface Copy Impacts Decision Making

- **Author / organisation:** Lexie Kane, Nielsen Norman Group
- **URL:** https://www.nngroup.com/articles/interface-copy-decision-making/
- **Publication date:** 2019-02-10
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** Interface wording affects decisions by explaining choices, consequences, and next steps.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Copy near controls should reduce uncertainty and tell users what will happen.
- **One Yugya application:** Make form status, CTA labels, and demo instructions explicit; for example, keep the current honesty that the enquiry tool prepares an email draft and sends nothing by itself.
- **Evidence type:** Expert UX guidance
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

## GOV.UK Service Manual and publishing guidance

### S12. Design — GOV.UK Service Manual

- **Author / organisation:** Government Digital Service, GOV.UK
- **URL:** https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design
- **Publication date:** Not shown on the fetched page
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Professional recommendation:** Service design covers the whole user problem, scoping, prototyping, inclusive patterns, and clear content.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Writing for interfaces and designing good questions are treated as part of design, not as late-stage decoration.
- **One Yugya application:** Treat FAQ wording and enquiry form questions as service design: ask only what is needed, in the user's language, at the point where the answer helps.
- **Evidence type:** Government service-design guidance
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

### S13. Writing for user interfaces

- **Author / organisation:** Government Digital Service, GOV.UK
- **URL:** https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/writing-for-user-interfaces
- **Publication date:** Published 2017-10-10; last updated 2018-04-16
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Professional recommendation:** Use users' language, keep sentences short and direct, reduce cognitive load, and write inclusive interface text.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Content should help people complete the task rather than reflect internal team terminology.
- **One Yugya application:** Replace internal headings such as "engagement scope" or "phased build & audits" in future copy with plain descriptions of what the visitor gives, receives, and approves.
- **Evidence type:** Government service-design guidance
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

### S14. Designing good questions

- **Author / organisation:** Government Digital Service, GOV.UK
- **URL:** https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/designing-good-questions
- **Publication date:** Published 2018-06-22; last updated 2026-06-24
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Professional recommendation:** Ask questions users understand, explain why information is needed, and provide help when the answer is not obvious.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Consider question order, wording, and whether each question is necessary.
- **One Yugya application:** Review the enquiry checklist and form labels against what a first-time buyer can actually answer, then add short help text in future copy rather than asking for technical categories.
- **Evidence type:** Government service-design guidance
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

### S15. Style guides

- **Author / organisation:** Government Digital Service, GOV.UK
- **URL:** https://www.gov.uk/guidance/style-guide
- **Resolved URL:** https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/style-guides/
- **Publication date:** Not shown on the fetched page
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Professional recommendation:** GOV.UK maintains separate general and technical style guidance, showing that consistent vocabulary and deliberate exceptions both matter.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Style guidance belongs to a wider writing standards system, not to isolated word choices.
- **One Yugya application:** Keep `yugya-brand-voice.md` as the single source for approved voice, vocabulary, and retired phrases rather than letting each page invent its own terminology.
- **Evidence type:** Government editorial guidance
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

## CXL

### S16. Unique Value Proposition: How to Create a UVP (With 7 Examples)

- **Author / organisation:** Peep Laja, CXL
- **URL:** https://cxl.com/blog/value-proposition/
- **Publication date:** Published 2022-12-16; last updated 2026-11-05
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Professional recommendation:** A strong value proposition names the audience and outcome, is specific enough to be meaningful, and is supported by evidence.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Generic feature lists and AI-assisted drafts can produce vague, interchangeable wording; customer language and testing improve specificity.
  - **Third-party opinion:** The article's examples illustrate the structure but do not establish what will work for a small Nepalese studio.
- **One Yugya application:** Audit future headlines with a "customer, outcome, difference, evidence" test before approving any new value statement.
- **Evidence type:** Practitioner conversion guidance with examples
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

### S17. Landing page optimization process for high conversion rates

- **Author / organisation:** Gerda Vogt-Thomas, CXL
- **URL:** https://cxl.com/blog/landing-page-optimization/
- **Publication date:** Published 2024-06-13; last updated 2026-11-10
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Professional recommendation:** Define the page goal and audience first, then align headline, proof, form, and CTA around that single goal.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Maintain message match between the source of the visit and the landing page; reduce form friction, support mobile layout, and test changes against a baseline.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Put evidence near the claim it supports and remove distractions from the conversion path.
- **One Yugya application:** For each future service or campaign page, name one primary action and keep proof, FAQ, and CTA focused on helping the visitor take it.
- **Evidence type:** Practitioner conversion guidance
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

### S18. Use Voice-of-Customer Research to Boost Conversions

- **Author / organisation:** Jennifer Havice, CXL
- **URL:** https://cxl.com/blog/voice-of-customer/
- **Publication date:** Published 2020-06-21; last updated 2024-06-17
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Professional recommendation:** Interviews, surveys, reviews, and forums reveal the words customers use for needs, priorities, and friction; those words can guide copy and test hypotheses.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Start with a clear decision to be influenced, gather open-ended responses, and map recurring messages by importance.
  - **Third-party opinion:** The article's reported case-study lifts show that message mining can work in those contexts; they are not predictions for Yugya.
- **One Yugya application:** Collect real enquiry language and founder-recorded buyer questions, then use those exact themes in future FAQ and headline tests without inventing quotes.
- **Evidence type:** Practitioner research guidance with case studies
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

### S19. Conversion rate optimization (CRO): the complete guide

- **Author / organisation:** CXL; author not shown on the fetched page. The guide credits multiple practitioners in its chapter list.
- **URL:** https://cxl.com/blog/conversion-rate-optimization/
- **Publication date:** Not shown on the fetched page
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Professional recommendation:** CRO raises the share of existing visitors who take a business-relevant action. The metric is useful but should serve business growth rather than become the goal by itself.
  - **Professional recommendation:** The process is research, hypothesis, prioritisation, testing where traffic allows, and recorded learning. Fix broken or unclear experiences before fine-tuning.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Be clear before clever, remove friction and distraction, and place proof where doubt is highest. Do not copy another site's winning test because its audience and offer differ.
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** Low-traffic sites can still do research-led CRO; statistical A/B testing requires enough conversions to produce a reliable result.
- **One Yugya application:** Treat a qualified enquiry as the primary conversion, gather qualitative evidence first, and avoid claiming A/B-test results without enough traffic.
- **Evidence type:** Practitioner conversion guide
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

### S20. Baymard UX Knowledge Hub

- **Author / organisation:** Baymard Institute
- **URL:** https://baymard.com/blog

### S21. Dark Patterns in UX: The Hidden Cost to Ecommerce Conversion (and the Deceptive Practices to Avoid)

- **Author / organisation:** Carl MacDonald, Baymard Institute
- **URL:** https://baymard.com/blog/dark-deceptive-patterns-ecommerce-ux
- **Publication date:** 2026-10-20
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** Deceptive interface patterns can produce short-term metric gains but damage trust and longer-term user behaviour.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Avoid hidden costs, forced choices, disguised advertising, false urgency, and designs that steer users against their interests.
  - **Third-party opinion:** The article's ecommerce framing should be translated cautiously to a service-studio enquiry flow.
- **One Yugya application:** Keep the site's honest form status, labelled demos, and clear boundaries; do not add countdowns, fake scarcity, hidden charges, or forced account creation.
- **Evidence type:** Industry research-informed UX guidance
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

### S22. Cart Abandonment Rate: 50 Statistics & Why People Abandon (2026)

- **Author / organisation:** Richard Lam, Baymard Institute
- **URL:** https://baymard.com/blog/cart-abandonment-rate
- **Publication date:** Published 2026-10-28; updated 2026-10-28
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Evidence-backed finding:** Baymard reports an average cart abandonment figure of about 70.22% and lists reasons including extra costs at about 40%, forced account creation at about 18%, credit-card trust concerns at about 19%, and checkout being too long or complicated at about 17%.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Remove unnecessary friction, reveal costs early, reduce security concerns, and make the completion path shorter and clearer.
  - **Third-party opinion:** These are ecommerce findings, not Yugya performance data or predictions for a Nepalese service enquiry.
- **One Yugya application:** Use the friction pattern as a form-review prompt: explain the mailto process, avoid unnecessary fields, and answer cost and ownership questions before the visitor reaches the form.
- **Evidence type:** Industry benchmark and survey synthesis
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17

## HubSpot

### S23. How to create a landing page with high ROI [+ expert and data-backed tips]

- **Author / organisation:** Hannah Harris, HubSpot
- **URL:** https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/landing-page-best-practices
- **Publication date:** Updated 2025-09-17
- **Key findings (paraphrased):**
  - **Professional recommendation:** Before writing, decide why the page exists, who it is for, and what one action the visitor should take.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Keep layout and copy focused on that action; use specific proof, clear CTA labels, and a short form.
  - **Professional recommendation:** Treat a landing page as a first draft and improve it through A/B testing where there is enough traffic.
  - **Third-party opinion:** HubSpot's product recommendations and cited marketing research reflect its platform and audience; they should not be repeated as Yugya-specific facts.
- **One Yugya application:** For any future campaign or service landing page, write the audience and single goal at the top of the brief before drafting copy or choosing a CTA.
- **Evidence type:** Vendor marketing guidance with cited research
- **Access date:** 2026-11-17
