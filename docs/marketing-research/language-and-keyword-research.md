# How Nepalese customers describe website and software services

**Prepared for:** Yugya Technologies (https://www.yugya.com/)
**Date:** 2026-11-17
**Scope:** Language and keyword research only. No app/ or components/ files were changed.
**Market:** Nepal, English + Devanagari Nepali + romanised Nepali.

## Method and honesty note

The built-in `websearch` tool returned HTTP 402/429 in this session, so I used `webfetch` against search-engine result pages (DuckDuckGo HTML and Lite endpoints, which approximate Google-style queries) and fetched the underlying Nepali pages directly. DuckDuckGo is **not** Google: results are a snapshot, not a volume or ranking forecast.

- **No paid keyword tool** (Keyword Planner, Ahrefs, Semrush) was connected.
- **No search volume, difficulty, CTR or traffic figures are claimed anywhere in this document.**
- Where a price appears below, it is quoted from a competitor's published page **as an observation of their language**, not as a Yugya price and not as a verified market rate. Yugya must not copy or repeat those numbers.
- `[E]` = **Evidence**: the exact phrase or a near-identical phrase was observed in a fetched page or search result on 2026-11-17.
- `[H]` = **Hypothesis**: plausible but not verified in the content reviewed. Treat as a question for Search Console or a customer conversation, not as a finding.

Existing internal research was read first: `docs/seo/keyword-research.md`, `docs/seo/keyword-to-url-map.md` and `docs/seo/content-strategy.md`. This document extends that work; it does not replace it.

## 7. Recommendation: stay English, improve plain-English clarity, defer Nepali pages

### Decision

**Keep the public site English.** Do **not** add a romanised-Nepali layer. Do **not** create a full Nepali mirror now. Revisit a *single*, human-written Nepali resource only after Yugya has its own query/ enquiry data.

### Evidence for this decision

1. `[E]` The commercial supplier landscape observed is overwhelmingly English — agency homepages, pricing guides, FAQs, directories and the national domain registry. Yugya's current English targets match that landscape.
2. `[E]` Nepali-language pages exist and use natural terminology (`वेबसाइट बनाउने कम्पनी नेपाल`, `वेबसाइट मूल्य नेपाल`, `सफ्टवेयर कम्पनी नेपाल`), but the sample found them on one solo developer's `/np` site, one DIY builder's `/ne` site, education blogs and news/government sites — not on a broad set of established agencies.
3. `[E]` Romanised website phrases in the observed results are concentrated in DIY tutorials, YouTube videos, Facebook posts and one informal business name. No commercial comparison page using romanised Nepali was found.
4. `[E]` Yugya's own live clients already span the language divide: a Nepali-language news site (Samajik Abhiyaan) and a bilingual preschool site (Aama Pathshala) [S1]. So Nepali-language *delivery* is proven; Nepali-language *marketing copy* is a separate, unproven channel.
5. `[E]` Existing internal guidance already states the technical constraints: no machine-translated pages, title language must match page content, the Google Translate widget is a convenience and not multilingual SEO (`keyword-research.md` §7).
6. `[E]` Maintenance burden is concrete: a Nepali mirror would double every service section, FAQ, metadata block, sitemap entry and internal link, and would need `lang="ne"`, human review, hreflang-style signalling and re-running `npm run seo:check`. The project's parity contract ("same words, same markup") applies to the existing English pages; adding a second language is a new project, not a copy edit.

### Hypotheses to keep open

- `[H]` Some Nepali-speaking buyers search in Devanagari or romanised Nepali and cannot express the need in English. Plausible; unmeasured in Yugya's data.
- `[H]` A Nepali-speaking owner may brief in Nepali and then read an English proposal. Plausible given the founder/client context; unmeasured.
- `[H]` Romanised phrases convert poorly in polished copy because they read as informal or keyword-stuffed. This is a judgement about brand fit, **not** a measured conversion finding.

### Practical sequencing

1. **Now:** improve plain-English clarity on the existing English pages using §8. No new pages, no new colours, no new copy claims.
2. **Now:** keep the footer Google Translate widget as a convenience only; never describe it as a Nepali-language site.
3. **Next 3–6 months:** collect Google Search Console queries (country = Nepal), the `?service=` enquiries, and the questions founders actually hear. Record Devanagari and romanised queries separately.
4. **Only if data justifies it:** publish *one* genuinely useful, human-written Nepali page with its own URL, `lang="ne"`, reviewed by a named fluent speaker, and internal links from the English site. Good first candidates, in order: a plain `.com.np` domain guide, then "what to prepare before asking for a website quote". Do **not** translate the whole site or create `/ne/` clones of every page.
5. **Never:** machine-translated pages, romanised landing pages, or mixed Nepali-English sentences inside Yugya's existing polished copy. Code-switching is observed in competitors' marketing [S6][S19][S21]; copying it would break the site's established voice.

## 8. Plain-English vocabulary guide for Yugya copy

Rule: keep the precise technical term **once**, with a plain explanation immediately after it, when the reader needs it. Do not replace accuracy with vague marketing.

| Phrase to avoid or qualify | Customer-friendly alternative | Where to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "web development company in Nepal" | "We build websites for businesses in Nepal." | `/` title/H1 area and metadata; one clear statement, not repeated verbatim |
| "responsive website" | "a site that works properly on a phone" | `/services/#web-development`, demo intros |
| "web application" | "a tool your team uses in a browser" | `/services/#web-development`, `/work/campusflow/`, `/work/stockwise/` |
| "online presence" (as a vague promise) | "a site people can find and trust, not just a social page" | `/` and `/services/` intro only, if needed — phrase is used in Nepal marketing [S21] but is not a claim |
| "SEO-friendly" / "AEO" / "GEO" | "built so people can find you on Google when they search for your service" | `/services/#web-development`; keep acronyms out of headings |
| "Linux VPS setup and server infrastructure" | "your own server, set up and documented" | `/services/#server-infrastructure`, `/work/#server-infrastructure` |
| "Caddy/Nginx reverse proxy" | "the part that receives visitors and sends them to the right site" (technical name in parentheses) | Server scope, build notes |
| "Git-based deployment" | "updates go through a recorded, repeatable process" | `/services/#server-infrastructure`, handover copy |
| "domain and DNS guidance" | "your web address, and making it point to the right place" | `/services/#domain-dns`, `/contact/?service=dns` |
| "SPF/DKIM/DMARC records" | "settings that help your business email arrive instead of being rejected" | `/services/#domain-dns` |
| "business automation / Python scripts" | "small scripts that take care of the repetitive parts of your paperwork" | `/services/#automation`, `/work/worknest/` |
| "AI integration" | "AI only where it removes a repeatable step; a person still checks the result" | `/services/#automation`, Yugs section — do not sell a service that is not scoped |
| "ongoing maintenance" | "we check updates, backups, forms and the padlock every month" | `/services/#maintenance`, `/contact/?service=maintenance` |
| "TLS renewal" | "keeping the site's padlock valid" | Maintenance FAQ |
