# Yugya brand voice

Date: 2026-11-17
Status: strategy for the 2026 copy transformation. Not a design document.

---

## 1. The voice in one sentence

Yugya sounds like a capable local technologist explaining the work to a business owner across a table: plain, specific, calm, and willing to say what it does not do.

## 2. Why this voice

| Input | Finding | Voice consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Competitor research (`competitor-analysis.md`) | The observed market defaults to keyword-stuffed H1s, superlatives ("best", "No.1", "most reliable"), self-reported count stacks, WhatsApp-first CTAs, and almost no explanation of ownership or aftercare. | Do not compete on volume of adjectives. Compete on clarity and verifiable specifics. |
| Competitor research | One observed studio (Aurora Studios) uses honest small numbers and named people and reads as unusually credible. | Small and local is an asset. Do not inflate. |
| Language research (`language-and-keyword-research.md`) | English technology words (website, domain, hosting, SEO, app) are in normal Nepali usage; Romanised Nepali searches skew DIY/informal; no keyword-volume data exists. | Keep the public site in English, explain each technical term once in ordinary words, and do not force Nepali phrases into polished copy. |
| Verified business policies (`AGENTS.md`, `docs/live-projects.md`) | Written itemised quote, milestone billing, client-owned hosting, full handover, 30-day bug-fix window, 1–2 business day response aim. | These are voice assets. State them early and exactly. |
| Existing design contract (`AGENTS.md`) | Design, layout, palette, motion and markup structure are owner-approved. | Voice changes must fit existing components. No new visual language is needed. |

## 3. Voice attributes

### 3.1 Clear before clever
Lead with the thing the visitor gets. A shop owner should understand the opening line in one read.

- Avoid: "Technology built for the work that matters."
- Use: "Websites for businesses in Nepal."

### 3.2 Human, not corporate
Write to one person. Use "you" for the reader and "we" for Yugya. Prefer short, complete sentences. A technical term may appear, but only after its everyday meaning.

- Avoid: "Engagements are structured around explicit operational requirements."
- Use: "We agree the pages or features, the content you will provide and the cost before work starts."

### 3.3 Confident without swagger
Confidence comes from specifics: real project names, a written quote, a named response aim, a stated handover. Never from "leading", "world-class" or "trusted by hundreds".

- Avoid: "We deliver world-class digital ecosystems."
- Use: "We built and handed over these sites. You can open them now."

### 3.4 Honest about limits
Say what is in scope, what is scoped separately, and what is out of scope. Keep the existing boundaries table. Do not imply 24/7 support, fixed prices, guaranteed rankings, or unlimited revisions.

### 3.5 Technically capable, not showy
Technical detail belongs in deliverables, build notes and diagrams — after the plain explanation. A startup founder should still see depth; a first-time buyer should not need it to decide.

## 4. Vocabulary rules

| Technical term | First-use plain explanation | Acceptable later shorthand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Domain | "your web address (like `yourbusiness.com.np`)" | domain |
| Hosting / server | "the computer where your website lives" | hosting, server |
| DNS | "the settings that point your web address to the right place" | DNS |
| Responsive | "works on phones, tablets and computers" | mobile-friendly |
| SEO | "helping the right people find the site when they search" | search visibility |
| Maintenance | "regular checks, updates and small fixes" | maintenance |
| Handover | "we give you the files, access and documents" | handover |
| Web application | "a site that does something, like accounts, bookings or a logged-in area" | web app |
| Automation | "a small tool that does a repeated job for you" | automation |

Rules:
1. Explain once, near the first mention on a page, then use the familiar word.
2. Never define a word by using a more unfamiliar word ("a domain is a DNS identifier").
3. Keep brand and product names as they are: Yugya, Yugya Technologies, Yugs, Kohinoor Restaurant, Samajik Abhiyaan, Aama Pathshala.
4. Keep the required demo captions and `DEMO_DISCLAIMER` verbatim. Do not paraphrase sample-data labelling.
5. Keep verified policies verbatim in substance: 30 calendar days, milestone billing, itemised written quote, client-owned accounts, direct email/messaging in business hours, 1–2 business day response aim.

## 5. Phrases to retire

These are not forbidden words in all contexts, but they must not be used as unearned claims in Yugya copy:

best, No.1, world-class, leading, cutting-edge, revolutionary, seamless, future-ready, next-generation, digital transformation, unleash, transform, empower, revolutionise, game-changing, unparalleled, digital excellence, complete digital backbone, 100% satisfaction, guaranteed results, guaranteed uptime, trusted by [number] businesses, [number]+ projects, [number]+ years (unless verified and approved).

Also retire internal-process language from customer-facing headings: "operational requirement", "itemized deliverables", "technical execution", "architecture & design", "phased build & audits", "technical requirements", "engagement scope", "digital front door", "systems that remain operational".

## 6. Tone by surface

| Surface | Tone | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home hero | Direct, one clear promise | "Websites for businesses in Nepal." |
| Service intros | Explanatory, benefit first, technical second | "A website gives your business its own place online…" |
| Service deliverables | Concrete, verifiable, no adjectives | "A finished site checked on phones, tablets and computers" |
| Portfolio | Let the work speak; factual descriptions only | "A live website for Kohinoor Restaurant…" |
| About | Personal and modest | "Two founders work directly with clients from the first message to handover." |
| Contact | Welcoming, low-friction | "You do not need a technical brief. A rough idea is enough." |
| Form status | Reassuring, implementation-honest | "Your email draft is ready. Nothing has been sent yet." |
| FAQ | Short answer first, then conditions | "It depends on the pages and features. You will get a timeline in the written quote." |
| Demos | Precise and labelled | "Sample data · runs in your browser" (verbatim) |
| 404 | Calm, useful | "This page does not exist." |

## 7. Mechanics

- Prefer sentences under 20 words. Long sentences are allowed for a genuine qualification, not for atmosphere.
- Use contractions ("you'll", "we'll", "don't") in body copy. Keep them out of legal/policy wording where precision matters.
- Use "free account" only when the product fact is verified (Yugs), never "free website".
- Use numerals for counts and time: "30 days", "1–2 business days", "five demos".
- Avoid exclamation marks on the public site.
- Buttons name the action: "Send an enquiry", "Visit live website", "Open email draft", "Discuss a build". No "Learn More" or "Click Here".
- Do not use a question as an H1 when a statement will explain faster. Questions remain useful in FAQ summaries.

## 8. Voice test before publishing any sentence

1. Could a shop owner who has never bought a website understand it on the first read?
2. Does it say what the customer gets, not what the vendor feels?
3. Is every number, name and capability in it verified?
4. Does it still sound like a small team a customer could email directly?
5. Have we kept the technical detail as support rather than the headline?

If any answer is no, rewrite.
