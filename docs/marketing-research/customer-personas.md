# Customer personas — Yugya Technologies

Date: 2026-11-17

These are **working personas**, not reported research participants. They consolidate (a) Nepal-specific observations in `nepal-market-analysis.md`, (b) the market patterns in `competitor-analysis.md`, (c) the audience list in the project brief, and (d) verified Yugya capabilities and policies. Where a detail is not directly evidenced, it is labelled **Hypothesis**. No persona is a invented interview, quote, statistic or customer.

Label key: **[E]** evidence from research or the verified site/policies · **[P]** professional recommendation derived from that evidence · **[H]** hypothesis to validate with real customers.

---

## 1. Segment map

The brief asks for six segments. They map to four buying personas because several segments share the same decision logic.

| Segment from brief | Primary persona | Secondary needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retail shops | A — Local shop owner | Product photos, contact, location, hours |
| Restaurants and hotels | B — Restaurant / hotel owner | Menu or rooms, gallery, location, booking enquiries |
| Educational institutions | D — Organisation / education | Information pages, announcements, forms, bilingual content |
| NGOs and community organisations | D — Organisation / education | Programme information, credibility, forms, reporting |
| Professional service providers / consultancies | C — Startup / growing company | Credibility, service pages, contact capture |
| Startups and growing companies | C — Startup / growing company | Web app or MVP, integrations, automation |

**[P]** One homepage must serve A and B in seconds while still signalling C and D. The mechanism is a plain H1/lead plus a short list of concrete proof, followed by deeper technical detail further down — not a vague phrase intended to mean everything to everyone.

## Persona A — Local shop owner

*Example context: owns a physical retail shop in a town outside Kathmandu; sells to walk-in customers and through Facebook/WhatsApp.* **[E: brief + market research context]**

1. **Primary goal.** Be findable and contactable by people who are not already followers; show products and shop details in one stable place.
2. **Biggest concerns.** "Will anyone actually see it?", "Will I be able to change prices/photos?", "What will I keep paying every year?", "Is this person trustworthy?", "What if it breaks and I can't reach anyone?" **[E/H — observed objections in market research; intensity is hypothesis]**
3. **Technical understanding.** Uses a smartphone, Facebook, possibly Instagram/TikTok and WhatsApp. May not know what a domain or hosting is. Does not need to. **[E]** "Facebook is enough" is a common starting position. **[H]**
4. **Likely objections.** Upfront cost; fear of a recurring bill nobody explained; thinking a website will not bring customers; not wanting to learn a content system; uncertainty about who owns the site after payment.
5. **What makes them trust a provider.** A real site they can open on a phone; a named local business in the same country; named people; a written quote; a plain explanation of what happens after launch. **[E: competitor gap analysis + Yugya assets]**
6. **Evidence they need.** The live restaurant and preschool sites; a sentence saying what the customer owns; a sentence saying what recurring costs exist; the response aim; the absence of fake claims.
7. **Language they understand.** "A place online for your business", "your web address", "customers can see your products and call you", "works on any phone". Technical words only after the plain meaning. **[E: language research]**
8. **Most appropriate CTA.** "Tell us what you need" / "Send an enquiry" — not "Book a technical consultation".
9. **Information shown before contact.** What a website does, what the customer provides (photos, text), what they own, what it costs to run (without inventing a price), what support exists after launch, and whether it works on a phone.

**Message to this persona (draft, to be finalised in `final-copy-strategy.md`):**
> "A website gives customers a place to see what you sell, find your location and call you — on any phone. We explain the costs, hand over everything, and help you update it later."

## Persona B — Restaurant or hotel owner

*Example context: runs a restaurant with a Facebook page, menu photos posted as images, and phone bookings. Kohinoor Restaurant is a verified live example of this business type.* **[E]**

1. **Primary goal.** Show menu, rooms/cabins, photos, location and contact clearly; make it easy for a visitor to call or find the venue. **[E: verified visible sections on the live Kohinoor site]**
2. **Biggest concerns.** Whether customers can find the site; keeping menu and prices current; photo quality; whether online enquiries or bookings are needed now or later; being tied to a platform's rules. **[H except where market research observed delivery-app/platform reliance]**
3. **Technical understanding.** Comfortable with social media and delivery apps. Likely to ask "Can you make something like our Facebook page, but proper?" **[H — plain-language formulation from language research, not a quote]**
4. **Likely objections.** "Our menu changes often", "we already get orders through apps", "will this work on a customer's phone", "who updates it when I am busy?"
5. **What makes them trust a provider.** A restaurant site they can open and compare against; real screenshots including mobile; no promise of booking numbers or revenue.
6. **Evidence they need.** Kohinoor Restaurant shown early with a live link; a plain note that menu/gallery/location/contact pages are ordinary website work; a clear answer to "can I update it later?".
7. **Language they understand.** "Menu, photos, location and phone number on one site", "works on a phone", "easy to update".
8. **Most appropriate CTA.** "Show me a similar site" leading to `/work/#kohinoor-restaurant`, then "Tell us what your menu needs".
9. **Information shown before contact.** A relevant live example, what pages are typical, whether booking forms are possible (without claiming a specific integration), what the customer provides, and the update/support answer.

**Message to this persona (draft):**
> "Show your menu, rooms, photos, location and phone number in one place. See a restaurant site we built, then tell us what yours needs."

## Persona C — Startup founder / growing company

*Example context: technical enough to know what an MVP or integration is, but wants a provider who will not overpromise or disappear after launch.* **[E: brief]**

1. **Primary goal.** A credible company site plus, where needed, a web application, internal tool or AI-assisted workflow — scoped realistically.
2. **Biggest concerns.** Whether the team can actually build the harder part; whether the code will be maintainable; whether they will own the repository and infrastructure; whether the vendor will inflate scope or claim experience it lacks. **[E/H — partly inferred from competitor trust patterns and verified Yugya policies]**
3. **Technical understanding.** Medium to high. Recognises terms like API, deployment, VPS, database. Does not want marketing fog.
4. **Likely objections.** "Is this a real engineering team or a template reseller?", "Will I get the repo?", "Can you integrate with what we already use?", "What happens when requirements change?"
5. **What makes them trust a provider.** Technical build notes, labelled demos with real in-browser logic, named founders with public portfolios, precise scope boundaries, and honest statements about what is simulated. **[E: existing Yugya site assets]**
6. **Evidence they need.** `/work/#aayushai-core`, the server baseline, the interactive demos, service deliverables, and the handover policy.
7. **Language they understand.** Both plain outcome language and accurate technical terms. They appreciate a short plain statement followed by real detail.
8. **Most appropriate CTA.** "Discuss a build" / "Review the service scope".
9. **Information shown before contact.** Capability depth, what is truly interactive in the demos, ownership/handover, scoping process, and a contact path that does not force a sales call.

**Message to this persona (draft):**
> "Need more than a brochure site? See the tools and systems we have built, try the demos, and read exactly what each one really does."

## Persona D — Organisation / educational institution

*Example context: a school, NGO or community organisation with several stakeholders, a budget approval step and a need for announcements, forms or bilingual content. Aama Pathshala and Samajik Abhiyaan are verified live examples of adjacent institution/media needs.* **[E]**

1. **Primary goal.** Publish trustworthy information; make it easy for parents, members or the public to find details and get in touch. Sometimes announcements, forms, archives or a language toggle. **[E: visible live-site features]**
2. **Biggest concerns.** Accuracy and tone of content; who maintains it after launch; decision-by-committee timelines; accessibility on the devices used by their community; whether a small vendor will still be reachable next year. **[H]**
3. **Technical understanding.** Low to medium. One staff member may be the "technical person" by default.
4. **Likely objections.** "We need approval", "our content is in Nepali", "who will update announcements?", "can it handle forms?", "what if the person who built it leaves?"
5. **What makes them trust a provider.** Real education/publication examples, a documented handover, direct named contacts, a written scope, and no claim of capabilities that would create risk.
6. **Evidence they need.** Aama Pathshala's bilingual site; Samajik Abhiyaan's category/article structure; a clear statement that files and access go to them; the maintenance option.
7. **Language they understand.** "Information pages, announcements, a contact form, a Nepali/English toggle where needed", then standard terms with explanation.
8. **Most appropriate CTA.** "Discuss what your institution needs" / "Send an enquiry".
9. **Information shown before contact.** Similar live examples, what content they must supply, what ongoing upkeep involves, who owns the accounts, and how communication works.

**Message to this persona (draft):**
> "Clear sites for schools and organisations: information, announcements, contact forms and Nepali/English pages where needed — with everything handed over to you."

## 2. Shared fears and the honest answer

| Fear | Plain answer to show on the site | Source of the answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hidden or recurring fees | Domain and hosting are paid directly by the client to the provider in their own account. Quote is itemised after scope review. No fixed packages published. | Verified policy |
| No ownership | Files, domain settings and server access handed over. | Verified policy |
| Nobody to call after launch | 30 calendar days bug fixing; optional monthly maintenance; direct email/messaging in business hours; aim to reply in 1–2 business days. Not a 24/7 call centre. | Verified policy |
| "Will it work on a phone?" | Layouts are checked on mobile, tablet and desktop. | Verified deliverable |
| "Can I update it?" | A written guide is provided; minor text updates are covered by the maintenance option. No claim of a visual CMS unless it is actually part of the agreed scope. | Verified policy |
| "Are these people real?" | Named founders with portraits and public portfolios; real client sites with live links. | Verified site assets |
| "Is the sample data real?" | No. Demos are labelled with `DEMO_DISCLAIMER` and "Sample data · runs in your browser" verbatim. | Verified policy |

## 3. What we deliberately do not claim

No testimonials, customer counts, revenue/conversion improvements, uptime guarantees, Google ranking guarantees, awards, certifications, partnerships, years of experience, fixed prices, fixed delivery times, or 24/7 support. If a persona would find such a signal reassuring, the answer is a verifiable substitute — a live site, a named person, a written policy — not an invented one.

## 4. Validation required

**[P]** These personas are a research synthesis, not customer research. Before treating any message as proven, validate with 5–8 real owners across A–D: ask what they currently use online, what they would pay for, which line of the homepage they would act on, and what would stop them enquiring. Record their own words; do not lead with Yugya's terminology. See `implementation-report.md` for the proposed validation plan.
