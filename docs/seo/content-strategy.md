## 1. Audit of existing content

| Content | Route | Value |

| Services | `/services/` | Five detailed service scopes with deliverables, constraints and CTAs |

### What does not exist

- No blog, article archive, RSS feed, tag pages, author pages or CMS.
- No glossary, pricing page, case-study detail pages beyond the work anchors, or standalone service pages.
- No Nepali-language content. The footer Google Translate widget is a convenience, not localized SEO.

### Decision

Do not create an empty blog or mass-produced articles. The existing content is stronger than a thin blog: it is specific, technical and tied to real builds. New content should extend it with genuine expertise, not pad the site.

No existing article was deleted or rewritten. The only visible copy changes were:

- one new pricing-basis FAQ on `/contact/`;
- one stale 404 sentence corrected ("five pages" → "main sections").

## 2. Editorial principles

1. **First-hand.** Write from actual delivery experience, real constraints and real technical decisions.
2. **Original.** Do not rewrite competitor articles with synonyms. If a source is used, cite it and add value.
3. **No invented facts.** No fabricated statistics, prices, timelines, client names, testimonials or credentials.
4. **Honest scope.** State what is not included and what depends on the client's decisions.
5. **People-first.** Organize for a reader who wants to make a decision, not for a keyword crawler.
6. **One intent per article.** Link to the relevant service, demo and contact page.
7. **Human review.** Any AI-assisted draft must be edited, fact-checked and approved by a named person before publishing.
8. **Design fit.** Reuse the existing typography, spacing and components. Do not introduce a generic blog template that breaks the brand.
9. **No doorway pages.** No city/service matrix pages without real local value.
10. **No fake freshness.** Do not change dates to pretend content is new.

## 5. Publishing process

1. **Brief approval.** Confirm the article is accurate, useful and within the studio's real services.
2. **Draft.** Write from first-hand experience. Mark uncertain facts with a source or a verification task.
3. **Fact-check.** Verify every number, date, legal requirement and technical claim. For `.np` domains, check register.com.np on the publication date.
4. **Edit.** Check clarity, structure, internal links, title, description and H1. Remove keyword repetition.
5. **Design.** Use existing components. Do not create a generic blog card grid that conflicts with the site.
6. **Author.** Use a real person with a real byline and, if an author page exists, a real biography. Do not invent credentials.
7. **Build and test.** Run `typecheck`, `lint`, `build`, `seo:check`. Check mobile layout and internal links.
8. **Publish and index.** Request indexing in Search Console. Do not claim ranking or AI inclusion.
9. **Review.** After 30–60 days, check Search Console queries and update the article if needed.

---

## 6. What not to publish

- A "top 10 web development companies in Nepal" list that ranks Yugya first without evidence.
- A price table copied from a competitor.
- A city page with only the city name changed.
- An AI-generated article with no first-hand input.
- A fake case study, testimonial or client logo.
- A `.com.np` guide that has not been checked against the registry.
- An "AI agency" page when the studio has not scoped that service.
- A blog archive with no articles.
